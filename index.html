GAME BREAKDOWN

TOKYO
DRIFT

Arcade Drifting in UE5
UE5BlueprintUE's Chaos VehiclesARCADE DRIFTINGJAPANESE CAR CULTURE
BP + C++LANGUAGE
UE5ENGINE
SOLOTEAM SIZE
THE IDEA
After coming back from Tokyo, I kept thinking about all the cool cars I saw there, and wanted to make a game about them. And since drifting originated in Japan, so a game centered around Japanese car culture just made sense. Then I thought about what would capture that feeling well, and based on what I saw and took photos off, I knew that I wanted a neon city with graffiti but haven't added it yet.. but for now I have a drift track with hills.
Also, before this I wanted to make a drifting game to see how I could build smooth drifting physics and how car physics actually work as I enjoyed playing these types of games. The drifting physics took a lot of iterations to make it feel right and to make it behave how a actual car would.
GAMEPLAY VIDEO

Gameplay footage showing the drift physics, car handling, environment and all the juicy effects and sound.

01
WHAT'S UNDER THE HOOD
no pun intended.
PHYSICS
Vehicle Physics
The base vehicle simulation is handled by UE5's Chaos Vehicle physics system. So Car has custom engine, transmission, differential setups and the wheels with lateral slip curves, suspension, and torque configs similar to their real world counterparts.
DRIFT SYSTEM
Arcade drifting
I wanted the drift to feel arcadey, so that required a component on top of Chaos physics to reduce the realism and make it fast paced. This system has counter steering, applies drift force, adjust torque and angular damping to get the arcade feel.
environment
drift track
Drift track as the environment of the game - japan themed with kanji and hiragana markings with mountain and nature environment, then edit to sustained drift angles around corners with wide sand runoff zones and cones placement.
scoring SYSTEM
Score
The Scoring system is designed to reward controlled and dedicated drifts with different multiplier based on which gear the car is on, score points depending upon tilt of the car with continuous drift meter drain.
CAMERA
Dynamic Spring Arm
The camera tilts and lags based on MPH and drift angle, and FOV change ofcourse. When the car is in a drift, the spring arm rotates dynamically to capture the drift perfectly. Camera system to change perspective from behind car to driver or rear perspective.
VFX & SFX
Skid marks & Engine sound
Niagara particle systems on each wheel emit skid marks based on drift angle and speed. Meta-sound system for the engine and skid sound, which changes based on RPM, drift angle.
02
CHALLENGES & SOLUTIONS
01
Chaos Physics Fighting Me on Rear Traction
UE5's Chaos vehicle system isn't built for drifting. No matter how much you tweak Chaos Vehicle's exposed parameters, it doesn't produce real drift. Chaos vehicle marks drifting as slip and reduces both lateral and sideways speed equally, resulting in dead drifts. After the physics tweak on top of the chaos physics and other tweaks inside chaos physics like center of mass, engine and wheel setup.
Instant slowing down when entering drift, resulting in dead drift
Dynamically change angular damping and apply force for arcade feel and better drift.
03
WHAT I LEARNED
ENGINE SOURCE DIVING
When the engine doesn't expose the parameters you need, you need to go read the source. Learned a lot about c++, how a big scalable system is build and to navigate UE5's souce code to find the files.
BLUEPRINT's LIMITS
Unreal Engine's visual scripting language - blueprint has it limitations not just in terms of performance but a lot of engine features aren't exposed to blueprints.
PARTICLE DETAIL MATTERS
Tying Niagara particle and engine sound intensity to game variables (slip angle, speed) made VFX and SFX feel reactive and alive. These effects should represent what's actually happening in gameplay.
CHAOS VEHICLE NOT BUILD FOR DRIFT
No matter how much you tweak Chaos Vehicle's exposed parameters, it doesn't produce real drift, unless you make your own function in C++. Chaos Vehicle shines for grip cars, suspension etc. Learned to build around that limitation.
04
WHAT I'D DO DIFFERENTLY
1
Extend Chaos Vehicle properly, with c++ and blueprint both. Use the Chaos Vehicle as the base for the animation, suspension and change the code that handles slip with my own in c++ and use blueprint for rapid iteration.
2
Build a better sound system. Engine audio is based on RPM with pitch changes, but I would like to build a system which plays sound on gear change as engine sound is one of the things I like the most about cars. But getting good sound is the hardest part.
2
Handle skid marks differently. Currently I'm using a ribbon renderer, but those appear twisted at some angles. I'm thinking of using just simple emitter to spawn planes, but they appear discontinuous unless spawning many planes, which affects performance.

Noroi

PS1 Style Survival Horror Game inspired by original Resident Evil & Silent Hill.
GodotGDScriptGDShaderRetro
GDScript + GDShaderLANGUAGE
GodotENGINE
SoloTeam Size
THE IDEA
I was watching a lot of retro games gameplay like Resident Evil, Silent Hill, Metal Gear and more. I became fascinated and obsessed with the unique visual of retro games, some that arose due to hardware limitation and some artistic choices - Vertex jitter, limited colors with dither, low poly models, using pre-rendered or scanned real-life textures etc.
For the game I planned to make a small prototype focused on visuals and polish. The PS1 visual is best for a horror game and given that horro game inspired me to make this game, so I made one.
I Tried to match the retro style as much as I can by not just having shaders to simulate the hardware limitations but also with limiting perspective with fixed camera. Later Section include code snippets and shader.
